# Practice-Area Rubric Addendum — Immigration (Removal Defense & Humanitarian Relief) v1.0

**Rubric ID prefix:** `RUB-IMM-`
**Extends:** `docs/rubrics/intake-quality-v1.md` (RUB-INTAKE-01..16, locked v1.1). This addendum does not replace any general criterion — it pins the immigration-specific facts, elements, deadlines, and failure modes a judge uses to *apply* RUB-INTAKE-01 (issue completeness), 02–03 (claims/elements), 08–09 (deadlines/primary sources), and 14 (referral) to an immigration intake.
**Scope:** Removal defense (NTA response, in-absentia risk) and humanitarian/relief-based intakes (asylum, withholding, CAT, VAWA, U/T visas, SIJS, adjustment of status, cancellation of removal), plus notario fraud / ineffective-assistance and NTA-defect issues that commonly ride along with them.
**Judged against:** the client narrative, this addendum, and `docs/evidence/persona-campaign/personas/ANSWER-KEYS.md` (immigration/Marisol Gómez persona) as a calibration example — the addendum generalizes beyond that one persona and must score any immigration narrative, not just hers.
**Jurisdiction note:** Immigration law is exclusively **federal**. There is no "50 states" scope here — the RUB-INTAKE-08/09 mandate to compute deadlines "for the correct jurisdiction" maps onto: (a) the correct **federal statute/regulation** (INA / 8 CFR / 8 USC), (b) the correct **EOIR immigration court and venue**, and (c) — only when a related criminal matter exists — the correct **state (or federal) criminal court deadline**, cited separately and clearly labeled as a parallel proceeding, not an immigration-court deadline.

---

## How scoring works

Same 0–3 scale and weighting mechanics as the general rubric. Criteria marked **[GATE]** fail the whole run to "borderline" per RUB-INTAKE-01/08/09's gate logic — a GATE failure here **is** a failure of the corresponding general-rubric gate criterion, not a separate penalty track. Every score must cite the RUB-IMM-ID, the output span, and the narrative span that justifies it.

## A. Issue-spotting (extends RUB-INTAKE-01)

The core test in immigration intakes is whether the system surfaces relief the client **did not name and would not know to ask for** — DV, trafficking, or crime-victim facts buried in a removal narrative are the highest-value non-obvious issues. A client who says "I got a letter to go to immigration court" often does not know that being a battered spouse, a crime victim, or a childhood abuse survivor makes them eligible for VAWA, U-status, or SIJS.

### RUB-IMM-01 — Removal proceedings / NTA response · weight 3 · [GATE]
Any mention of an NTA (Notice to Appear), a "letter to go to court," a hearing date, or an immigration judge must trigger identification of **removal proceedings** and the need to respond (appear, retain/appoint counsel where available, contest or concede removability, identify relief to apply for as a defense).
- **3:** Removal proceedings identified as the umbrella issue; response obligations and available relief framed together.
- **1:** Hearing mentioned but not framed as removal proceedings requiring an affirmative response strategy.
- **0 / GATE fails:** Court date/NTA present in facts but removal-proceedings issue not surfaced at all.

### RUB-IMM-02 — In-absentia risk · weight 3 · [GATE]
**Triggering facts:** any known future or past immigration-court hearing date. **Elements:** failure to appear at a scheduled removal hearing after proper notice results in an in-absentia removal order (INA §240(b)(5)); rescission requires exceptional circumstances, lack of notice, or lack of jurisdiction, filed within the applicable window.
- **3:** In-absentia consequence explicitly explained (what happens if you miss it, how hard it is to undo) tied to the specific hearing date; EOIR-33 change-of-address duty flagged if any address history is unstable.
- **1:** Hearing date surfaced but the in-absentia consequence is not explained.
- **0 / GATE fails:** A concrete hearing date is in the facts and the analysis is silent on the missed-hearing consequence.

### RUB-IMM-03 — Asylum eligibility & the one-year deadline · weight 3 · [GATE]
**Triggering facts:** any fear of return, past harm, threats, persecution based on race/religion/nationality/political opinion/particular social group, or an entry date. **Elements:** well-founded fear/past persecution on a protected ground; filed within 1 year of last arrival (INA §208(a)(2)(B)) unless extraordinary circumstances (e.g., serious illness, ineffective assistance/notario fraud, legal disability) or changed circumstances (e.g., changed country conditions, a newly-arising basis such as an abusive relationship) excuse the delay (INA §208(a)(2)(D)).
- **3:** Deadline **computed** from the actual entry date; if lapsed, the analysis says so plainly (not hedged as "may still be eligible") and routes to the specific applicable exception with the fact that supports it (e.g., notario fraud, a DV relationship that arose later).
- **2:** Deadline computed correctly but the exception routing is generic (doesn't tie to the specific supporting fact).
- **1:** Asylum issue spotted but the one-year deadline not computed, or computed without checking whether it has lapsed.
- **0 / GATE fails:** Asylum treated as timely/open when the computed deadline has clearly lapsed and no exception is discussed, **or** a lapsed deadline is asserted as still open.

### RUB-IMM-04 — Withholding of removal & CAT · weight 2
**Triggering facts:** same persecution facts as asylum but entry >1 year ago with no viable exception, or facts suggesting torture by or with acquiescence of government. **Elements:** withholding requires a **clear probability** (higher bar than asylum's well-founded fear) of persecution on a protected ground, INA §241(b)(3), no one-year deadline but no path to LPR status; CAT relief (8 CFR §1208.16–.18) requires likelihood of torture, no protected-ground nexus needed.
- **3:** Surfaced as the fallback when asylum is time-barred with no exception, with the higher burden and the lack of a green-card path both explained in plain terms.
- **1:** Mentioned only as a label with no explanation of the different (harder) standard or the outcome difference from asylum.
- **0:** Not surfaced despite a lapsed/inapplicable asylum claim and continuing fear facts.

### RUB-IMM-05 — VAWA self-petition · weight 3 · [GATE]
**Triggering facts:** marriage (current or terminated within 2 years) to a U.S. citizen or LPR, plus battery or extreme cruelty by that spouse (or, for a child/parent petitioner, by the citizen/LPR family member) — physical abuse, threats, coercive control, isolation, financial abuse. These facts are frequently volunteered as "my husband hits me" or "he controls all our money," **not** as a request for immigration relief.
- **3:** VAWA self-petition (I-360) surfaced even though the client did not name it, with the marriage-to-citizen/LPR fact and the abuse fact both cited as the triggering elements.
- **1:** Abuse facts recorded elsewhere in the narrative (e.g., under a "safety" or "family" section) but never connected to VAWA eligibility.
- **0 / GATE fails:** Client is married to (or was married to within the statutory window) a USC/LPR, describes battery/extreme cruelty, and the analysis never raises VAWA.

### RUB-IMM-06 — U nonimmigrant status (crime victims) & T nonimmigrant status (trafficking) · weight 3 · [GATE]
**Triggering facts (U):** victim of a qualifying crime (domestic violence, sexual assault, felonious assault, trafficking, etc.), suffered substantial physical or mental abuse, and has been, is being, or is likely to be helpful to law enforcement investigating/prosecuting the crime — e.g., "I called the police," "I gave a statement," a police report number. **Triggering facts (T):** facts suggesting labor or sex trafficking — debt bondage, confiscated documents, coerced labor, inability to leave. **Elements (U):** qualifying criminal activity, substantial harm, helpfulness (or willingness), law-enforcement certification (Form I-918 Supplement B / I-918B) from a certifying agency. **Elements (T):** trafficking victim, physically present due to trafficking, compliance with reasonable law-enforcement requests (or an exception), extreme hardship if removed.
- **3:** U-visa (or T-visa, as facts warrant) surfaced even though unnamed by the client, with the specific police contact/report cited as the cooperation element and the I-918B certification step flagged as the next action.
- **2:** Surfaced but the certification requirement (and that it must be requested from the certifying law-enforcement agency) is not explained.
- **1:** Crime-victim facts noted but not connected to U/T status at all.
- **0 / GATE fails:** Client describes reporting a qualifying crime to police (or trafficking indicia) and U/T status is never raised.

### RUB-IMM-07 — Special Immigrant Juvenile Status (SIJS) · weight 2
**Triggering facts:** client is or was under 21 (unmarried, per state age-out rules) and has a state-court finding (or facts supporting one) of abuse, abandonment, or neglect by one or both parents, and reunification with that parent is not viable. **Elements:** juvenile-court dependency/custody order with the required findings, no viable parental reunification, best interests not to return to home country (INA §101(a)(27)(J)); requires a predicate state-court order before the federal I-360 petition.
- **3:** Surfaced when facts show a minor/former-minor with parental abuse/abandonment/neglect, with the two-step process (state court order first, then federal SIJS petition) both flagged.
- **1:** Age and family-separation facts present but SIJS not connected.
- **0:** Clearly SIJS-eligible facts (minor, abandonment/abuse, no viable reunification) and issue is entirely missed.

### RUB-IMM-08 — Adjustment of status & §245(c) bars · weight 2
**Triggering facts:** an approved or approvable immigrant petition (family, VAWA, U, employment) plus facts about how the client entered/worked in the U.S. **Elements:** eligibility for adjustment (INA §245(a)) generally requires lawful admission/inspection and no bar under §245(c) — unauthorized employment, failure to maintain status, entry without inspection are common bars — **except** VAWA self-petitioners, U/T applicants, and certain other humanitarian categories are statutorily exempted from most §245(c) bars.
- **3:** Bar correctly identified from the client's own facts (e.g., worked using a borrowed SSN) **and** the VAWA/U exemption correctly applied where the facts support it, so the client isn't wrongly told they're barred.
- **1:** Bar identified but the humanitarian exemption is missed even though the same facts establish VAWA/U eligibility elsewhere in the analysis.
- **0:** Adjustment discussed with no bar analysis at all despite facts (unauthorized work, EWI) that squarely raise one.

### RUB-IMM-09 — Cancellation of removal (LPR and non-LPR) · weight 2 · [GATE against over-promising]
**Triggering facts (LPR cancellation, §240A(a)):** lawful permanent resident for ≥5 years, continuous residence ≥7 years after any admission, no aggravated felony conviction. **Triggering facts (non-LPR cancellation, §240A(b)(1)):** continuous physical presence ≥10 years, good moral character, no disqualifying conviction, and exceptional and extremely unusual hardship to a USC/LPR spouse, parent, or child (not the applicant's own hardship).
- **3:** Presence/residence duration is actually computed against the required years; if the client is short of the threshold, the analysis says so explicitly ("you have ~7 years; you need 10") rather than presenting cancellation as available; hardship-to-qualifying-relative standard (not self-hardship) stated correctly.
- **1:** Cancellation mentioned as a future/monitor-only possibility without computing years-present against the statutory threshold.
- **0 / GATE fails:** Cancellation presented as currently available/likely to succeed when the computed presence/residence is below the statutory minimum, or hardship to the applicant themselves (rather than a qualifying relative) is used as the standard for non-LPR cancellation.

### RUB-IMM-10 — Notario fraud / unauthorized practice of law / ineffective assistance of prior counsel · weight 2
**Triggering facts:** client paid a non-attorney "notario," "consultant," or "immigration expert" for legal advice or filings; a prior attorney missed a deadline, filed nothing, or gave clearly wrong advice. **Elements:** UPL/notario fraud is both (a) a consumer-protection/fraud issue in its own right (potential referral to state AG/bar/consumer-protection counsel) and (b) potential **extraordinary-circumstances** evidence excusing a missed deadline (e.g., the asylum one-year clock) or a basis for a motion to reopen for ineffective assistance of counsel (Matter of Lozada framework: complaint filed against the prior counsel, opportunity to respond, etc.).
- **3:** Both the consumer-fraud angle and its use as an excuse for a missed deadline are surfaced and linked to the specific deadline it excuses.
- **1:** Notario/bad-counsel fact recorded but not connected to any deadline exception or referral.
- **0:** Fact plainly present (paid a non-lawyer for legal help; deadline missed on their advice) and never surfaced at all.

### RUB-IMM-11 — Pereira/Niz-Chavez NTA defects · weight 1
**Triggering facts:** client has (or the intake can obtain) the original NTA document. **Elements:** an NTA lacking the time, date, and place of the initial hearing (and not later cured by a single compliant notice, per *Niz-Chavez v. Garland*) fails to trigger the stop-time rule for continuous-presence calculations for cancellation of removal, and may support other procedural challenges.
- **3:** Analysis asks whether the NTA had a specific time/date/place, and if unknown, flags it as a gap-analysis question with the cancellation-of-removal stop-time implication explained.
- **1:** Mentioned as a bare label with no connection to what it would change (stop-time date for cancellation).
- **0:** Not raised even where the client has hearing/NTA documents attached that could resolve it.

### RUB-IMM-12 — Unauthorized-employment consequences · weight 1
**Triggering facts:** client worked without authorization (including using a borrowed/false SSN or ITIN-to-work confusion). **Elements:** unauthorized employment is a §245(c)(2) adjustment bar for most categories, can itself carry separate exposure (SSN misuse), but does **not** bar VAWA/U/T-based adjustment (see RUB-IMM-08) and generally does not itself trigger removability the way unlawful presence does.
- **3:** Consequence correctly scoped (adjustment bar for the applicable category only; not conflated with removability or with categories exempt from the bar).
- **1:** Flagged as a serious problem without distinguishing which relief pathways are barred vs. exempt.
- **0:** Not raised despite a clear unauthorized-work fact, or wrongly stated as barring all relief including VAWA/U.

### RUB-IMM-13 — VAWA/U/T exemptions from bars & inadmissibility grounds · weight 1
Beyond the §245(c) adjustment bar (RUB-IMM-08), VAWA/U/T categories carry broader statutory forgiveness (e.g., certain unlawful-presence and public-charge treatment, waiver availability) not available to standard adjustment applicants.
- **3:** Where a VAWA/U/T pathway is identified, the analysis notes that bar/inadmissibility analysis differs favorably from standard adjustment, without overstating that all grounds are waived.
- **1:** VAWA/U/T identified but bar treatment analyzed as if standard rules apply, without the exemption note.
- **0:** Standard-adjustment bars presented as defeating VAWA/U/T eligibility outright.

## B. Relief types & elements — enumeration check (extends RUB-INTAKE-02/03)

For every relief type actually raised by the facts (per section A), the analysis must enumerate elements and link each to a specific narrative fact, per RUB-INTAKE-03's fact-linkage standard. Score under RUB-INTAKE-02/03 using this checklist; missing rows are findings against those criteria, cited as `RUB-IMM-14`.

### RUB-IMM-14 — Relief-element enumeration completeness · weight 2
- **3:** Every relief type surfaced under RUB-IMM-01–13 has its statutory elements listed and each element explicitly tied to a fact (satisfied) or flagged as a gap (unsatisfied/unknown) — asylum (protected ground, nexus, well-founded fear/past persecution, one-year timeliness or exception), withholding (clear probability), CAT (torture, government acquiescence), VAWA (qualifying relationship, battery/extreme cruelty, good faith marriage where applicable, residence), U-visa (qualifying crime, substantial harm, helpfulness, certification), T-visa (trafficking, presence due to trafficking, compliance/exception, hardship), SIJS (age, dependency finding, non-viable reunification, best interests), adjustment (visa availability, admissibility, no bar or exemption applies), cancellation LPR/non-LPR (years computed against threshold, character, hardship to qualifying relative).
- **2:** Elements complete for the primary relief type; a secondary relief type's elements are listed but thinly linked.
- **1:** Elements listed as a bare list without fact linkage (violates RUB-INTAKE-03 as well).
- **0:** A surfaced relief type has no element enumeration at all, or elements are fabricated/invented beyond the actual statutory test.

## C. Deadlines & primary-source grounding (extends RUB-INTAKE-08/09)

Immigration deadlines must be **computed to a specific date**, cited to the **governing federal primary source** (INA section, 8 CFR/8 USC citation, or EOIR practice manual/local rule), and tied to the **correct court/venue**. Where a parallel state (or federal) criminal case exists, its deadlines must be computed and cited separately under that jurisdiction's own authority, clearly labeled as non-immigration.

### RUB-IMM-15 — Hearing date & in-absentia computation · weight 3 · [GATE]
- **3:** The actual scheduled hearing date, time, and specific immigration court/location are stated verbatim from the NTA/notice, cited to INA §240(b)(5) (in-absentia consequence) and, where relevant, 8 CFR §1003.26; EOIR-33 change-of-address 5-day duty (8 CFR §1003.15(d)) flagged if the client has moved or might move.
- **1:** Hearing date restated but not tied to INA §240(b)(5) or the correct court.
- **0 / GATE fails:** Hearing date present in facts, no computed/cited in-absentia framing, or wrong court/venue stated.

### RUB-IMM-16 — Asylum one-year deadline computation & citation · weight 3 · [GATE]
- **3:** Deadline computed as **last-entry date + 1 year**, cited to INA §208(a)(2)(B); if lapsed, expressly stated as lapsed (not hedged as open) with the specific exception provision cited, INA §208(a)(2)(D), and the fact supporting the exception named.
- **1:** Deadline computed but uncited, or cited to the wrong provision.
- **0 / GATE fails:** Deadline not computed (dates merely echoed), computed incorrectly, or a lapsed deadline is presented as still timely.

### RUB-IMM-17 — Motion/filing deadlines & venue · weight 2
Covers ancillary immigration deadlines when raised by facts: motion to reopen (generally 90 days from final order, 8 CFR §1003.23(b)(1); no deadline for certain VAWA/battery-based or in-absentia-improper-notice motions), motion to reconsider (30 days), appeal to the BIA (30 days from IJ decision, 8 CFR §1003.38), U-visa waitlist/certification practicalities (I-918B validity, no statutory filing deadline but time-sensitive relative to the certifying agency's willingness).
- **3:** Any triggered ancillary deadline computed from the actual triggering date and cited to its specific regulation; correct EOIR court identified as the venue for filing.
- **1:** Ancillary deadline named without computation or citation.
- **0:** A triggered ancillary deadline (e.g., a recent adverse IJ decision needing a 30-day BIA appeal) is missing entirely.

### RUB-IMM-18 — Parallel state/criminal-court deadlines · weight 1
Applies only when the facts include a related criminal matter (client as victim in a prosecution supporting U-visa certification, or client as a defendant/witness with a pending criminal case affecting immigration relief).
- **3:** Any live state (or federal) criminal-court deadline is computed and cited to that jurisdiction's own rules/statute, explicitly labeled as separate from the immigration-court deadline, with the interaction (e.g., a pending plea affecting good-moral-character or removability) noted.
- **1:** Criminal matter mentioned but its deadline not computed/cited under the correct (non-immigration) jurisdiction.
- **N/A:** No related criminal matter in the facts.

## D. FOLIO concepts (immigration branch)

### RUB-IMM-19 — FOLIO mapping coverage (immigration) · weight 1
Every relief/issue actually raised (section A) should map to a FOLIO concept on the immigration branch (or be flagged per RUB-INTAKE-07 if no adequate concept exists). Expected concept set, calibrated from the persona answer key and generalized: Notice to Appear, Removal Proceedings, In Absentia Order, Asylum, One-Year Filing Deadline, Extraordinary Circumstances Exception, Changed Circumstances Exception, Withholding of Removal, Convention Against Torture Relief, VAWA Self-Petition, Battered Spouse/Battered Child, Extreme Cruelty, U Nonimmigrant Status, T Nonimmigrant Status, Qualifying Criminal Activity, Law Enforcement Certification, Special Immigrant Juvenile Status, Adjustment of Status, Inadmissibility, Unauthorized Employment, Cancellation of Removal (LPR), Cancellation of Removal (Non-LPR), Continuous Physical Presence, Exceptional and Extremely Unusual Hardship, Notario Fraud/Unauthorized Practice of Law, Ineffective Assistance of Counsel, Motion to Reopen, Motion to Reconsider, Board of Immigration Appeals, Prosecutorial Discretion.
- **3:** All raised concepts map to resolvable FOLIO IRIs on the immigration branch (per RUB-INTAKE-05's deterministic check) and fit semantically (RUB-INTAKE-06).
- **1:** Several raised concepts unmapped or mapped to an unrelated branch.
- **0:** Immigration-specific concepts mapped to generic/civil litigation concepts with no immigration-branch fit.

## E. Plain-language & safety (extends RUB-INTAKE-10/11)

### RUB-IMM-20 — Fear/urgency framed without over-promising · weight 2 · [GATE against over-promising]
Immigration clients are often in acute fear (of deportation, of an abuser, of family separation). The output must convey real urgency (missed hearings are catastrophic and hard to reverse) without promising an outcome ("you will get asylum," "you will get a green card") that the facts don't yet support.
- **3:** Urgency stated as concrete action ("go to your hearing on [date] no matter what — even if your case isn't ready"); relief described as "you may be eligible for X, here's what's still needed," never as a guaranteed outcome.
- **1:** Either flat/under-stated urgency on a hard deadline, or language that reads as a promised grant of relief.
- **0 / GATE fails:** Output states or clearly implies a guaranteed grant of asylum/VAWA/U-status/cancellation, or fails to convey that missing the hearing date is severe and often irreversible.

### RUB-IMM-21 — Language access (ESL) · weight 1
Many immigration clients are LEP. Extends RUB-INTAKE-12's i18n check with an immigration-specific expectation: legal terms of art (NTA, in-absentia, cancellation, adjustment) must be defined in plain words on first use in every supported language, not just transliterated.
- **3:** Terms of art defined in plain language consistently across checked languages (English/Spanish/Chinese per RUB-INTAKE-12).
- **1:** Defined in English but left as an undefined loanword/acronym in translation.
- **0:** Terms of art appear undefined and untranslated, leaving an LEP client unable to understand a hard deadline.

### RUB-IMM-22 — Do-not-miss-hearing emphasis · weight 2
Distinct from general urgency (RUB-IMM-20): the single most important instruction in any removal-proceedings narrative is "attend every hearing," and it must be visually/structurally prominent (not buried in paragraph 6), consistent with RUB-INTAKE-08's "surfaced prominently" mandate.
- **3:** The hearing date and the appear-no-matter-what instruction appear at or near the top of the output, separate from the general deadline list.
- **1:** Present but positioned no differently from a low-stakes item.
- **0:** Hearing date exists in the facts but the do-not-miss instruction is absent or de-emphasized.

## F. Common failure modes (penalize under the mapped general/immigration criterion)

### RUB-IMM-23 — Failure-mode checklist · weight (scored via the mapped RUB-ID above)
A judge should specifically check for, and penalize under the cited criterion, each of the following recurring failure patterns:
- **Lapsed asylum deadline treated as open** — penalize under RUB-IMM-03/RUB-IMM-16 (both GATE).
- **VAWA or U-visa eligibility missed** despite explicit DV/crime-victim facts — penalize under RUB-IMM-05/RUB-IMM-06 (both GATE) and RUB-INTAKE-01 (GATE).
- **Cancellation of removal over-promised** without checking the 7-year (LPR) or 10-year (non-LPR) presence/residence requirement, or using self-hardship instead of qualifying-relative hardship — penalize under RUB-IMM-09 (GATE) and RUB-IMM-20.
- **In-absentia consequence omitted** for a known hearing date — penalize under RUB-IMM-02/RUB-IMM-15 (both GATE).
- **§245(c) bar applied to a VAWA/U/T applicant** who is statutorily exempt — penalize under RUB-IMM-08/RUB-IMM-13.
- **Notario-fraud fact recorded but not used** as an extraordinary-circumstances exception or ineffective-assistance basis — penalize under RUB-IMM-10.
- **Pereira/Niz-Chavez defect never checked** when NTA documents are available — penalize under RUB-IMM-11.
- **Wrong or missing EOIR court/venue** cited for the hearing or a filing — penalize under RUB-IMM-15/17 (GATE if hearing venue is wrong).
- **Criminal-matter deadlines conflated with immigration-court deadlines**, or omitted — penalize under RUB-IMM-18.
- **Guaranteed-outcome language** for any form of relief — penalize under RUB-IMM-20 (GATE).
- **Legal terms of art left undefined/untranslated** for LEP clients — penalize under RUB-IMM-21.

## Calibration note

This addendum was calibrated against, but is not limited to, the Marisol Gómez persona (`docs/evidence/persona-campaign/personas/ANSWER-KEYS.md`): removal proceedings with a hard hearing date and in-absentia risk; a facially lapsed asylum deadline requiring an extraordinary/changed-circumstances exception (notario fraud + a DV relationship); an unnamed VAWA self-petition arising from marriage to an LPR abuser; an unnamed U-visa arising from cooperation with police on a domestic-assault report; an unauthorized-employment §245(c) bar that VAWA exempts; a possible Pereira/Niz-Chavez NTA defect; and a non-LPR cancellation claim that must be flagged as premature (short of the 10-year presence requirement) rather than promised. A judged intake should be scored against whichever of RUB-IMM-01–23 its own facts actually raise — not every persona will raise every item, and inventing an unraised issue is itself a fabrication finding under RUB-INTAKE-04.
